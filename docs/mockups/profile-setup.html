Setting up your profile, three ways

Today the profile is eight tabs of empty forms. These mockups show three replacements for setting it up by hand. In all three, Writing samples, the .tex template and backups move out to a new Settings page, so Profile holds only what goes on a resume.

Mockups use your real profile and Dovetailed's current look. They are pictures, not working screens.

A

Guided steps

A first-time setup that asks one thing per screen: you, work, projects, skills, education, then a check. The resume on the right fills in as you type, with the newest line highlighted, so you see what each answer is for.

Good

  • Nobody faces eight empty tabs: there is always exactly one next thing.
  • The live resume answers "why are you asking me this?"
  • Projects and Education are marked optional, and every step can be skipped.

Watch out

  • Slow for quick edits later, so returning users still need another view.
B

One page, edit in place

No tabs at all. Your profile is one sheet that reads like your resume. Click any entry and it opens for editing right where it sits; the outline on the left jumps between sections and flags anything missing, like a project with no bullets.

Good

  • You see everything at once, the way a recruiter will.
  • Editing a returning profile is one click, not tab, then entry, then field.
  • The same view works for setup and for later edits.

Watch out

  • An empty sheet is less guiding than steps for a brand-new user.
C

Talk it through

Describe a role in your own words, messy is fine. Dovetailed drafts the entry and shows which of your words each bullet came from, so nothing is invented. It also notices a second role you mentioned and offers to draft it.

Good

  • Fastest way to get bullets out of someone who hates writing them.
  • Every bullet is traced to your own words, which fits the "nothing invented" promise.
  • Reuses the notes-to-entry feature that already exists.

Watch out

  • One AI call per role on the shared free tier.
  • Some people would rather type into fields.
+

Settings, for everything that isn't your work history

Writing samples, the LaTeX template, backups and delete-all, each with one line on why it exists. Same in all three ideas.

What I'd build

B as the profile itself, with A's steps and C's "describe it" as ways to fill it.

  1. The profile becomes one page (B). No tabs. It reads like your resume, and you click to edit in place. This is what returning users see.
  2. A blank profile gets the guided steps (A). Same fields, one section at a time, with the resume filling in beside you. When you finish, you land on the one page.
  3. Every job and project step offers "Describe it instead" (C). Write it in your own words and get a traced draft. Typing fields stays the default.
  4. Settings gets its own page for samples, template and backups.
